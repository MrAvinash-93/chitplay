Checkers, also called draughts, is a classic game for two on the dark squares of a board. Each side starts with twelve pieces. ChitPlay plays English draughts rules, where a capture you can make must be made, which leads to neat traps.

Ways to play Checkers

  • 👤 Guest play Start straight away against computer players, no account and no sign-up. Choose 2 players.
  • 🌍 Play online Get matched with real players from around the world. If nobody is free within a few seconds, computer players take the empty seats so you are never left waiting.
  • 👫 Play with friends Open a room, and friends join from their own phones with a 6-digit code or by scanning a QR code. Works on the same Wi-Fi or across the internet.
  • 📱 One phone Pass one phone around the table; secrets stay hidden until it is your turn.
  • 👀 Watch Let the computer players show you how the game goes before you jump in.

How to play

  1. Pieces move one square diagonally forward onto a free dark square. Tap a piece, then a marked square.
  2. To capture, jump diagonally over a rival piece onto the free square behind it. The captured piece comes off.
  3. If you can capture you must, and if the same piece can then jump again, it must keep going.
  4. A piece reaching the far row becomes a king, ending that turn. Kings move and jump diagonally both forward and backward.

Scoring

A game ends in a win or a draw. The result table shows 1 for the winner.

Who wins

You win when the other player has no pieces or no legal move. Forty moves each without a capture or a man moving is a draw.

Tips

  • Keep your back row at home as long as you can: it stops rival kings being made.
  • Use compulsory captures against the other player: give up one piece to win two.
  • Pieces at the edge are safe from capture, but they have fewer moves.

Questions

Do you have to capture in checkers?

Yes. These rules make it compulsory, and the game only offers you capturing moves when one exists.

How does a piece become a king?

By reaching the far row. A crowned piece stops there and can move in both directions from the next turn.
