Chess is the great game of strategy for two, with roots in the Indian game Chaturanga. Each side has sixteen pieces and one aim: to trap the other king so that it can't escape. ChitPlay plays the full rules, and only ever offers you legal moves.

How to play

  1. White moves first, then the players take turns. Tap a piece, then tap a marked square.
  2. A rook moves in straight lines, a bishop along diagonals, a queen does both, a knight jumps in an L shape and a king moves one square.
  3. A pawn moves one square forward, or two on its first move, and captures one square diagonally forward. On the far row it becomes a queen, rook, bishop or knight.
  4. Castling: if the king and a rook haven't moved, the squares between are empty and the king doesn't pass through check, the king moves two squares towards the rook, which jumps over it.
  5. En passant: straight after a pawn moves two squares past your pawn, you may capture it as if it had moved one.
  6. A king under attack is in check. You must get out of check on your move.

Scoring

A game ends in a win or a draw. The result table shows 1 for the winner.

Who wins

Checkmate wins: the king is in check with no way out. A game is drawn by stalemate (no legal move, not in check), the same position three times, fifty moves each without a capture or pawn move, or too few pieces left to checkmate.

Tips

  • Control the centre early with pawns and bring out knights and bishops.
  • Castle early to keep your king safe.
  • Before each move, ask what the other player's last move is threatening.
  • Don't move the same piece twice in the opening without a good reason.
