Four folded chits, four players, one thief. The same game is played all over the world under different names: Raja Rani Chor Police and Chor Sipahi in India, Raja Wazir Chor Sipahi in Pakistan, Raja Mantri Chor Sipahi in Nepal, Cops and Robbers in the USA and UK, Räuber und Gendarm in Germany. ChitPlay plays it free in your browser, in 3D, under the name of the language you pick.

How to play

  1. Four chits are folded and thrown down: King (1000), Queen (800), Police (500) and Thief (0).
  2. Each player picks one and reads it secretly.
  3. The Police says who they are and has to find the Thief among the other three.
  4. The Police names a suspect, then everyone opens their chit.
  5. Right guess: the Police keeps 500 and the Thief gets 0. Wrong guess: the Thief takes the Police's 500. King and Queen always keep their points.
  6. Play as many rounds as you like; the highest total wins.

House rules from around the world

  • Japan: one innocent player is cleared before the Police guesses.
  • USA: a second guess for half the points.
  • UK: a wrong guess costs the Police 200 and the Thief escapes with 1000.
  • China: a caught Thief pays a 200-point fine.

Play it on paper

Tear a page into four equal slips, write King 1000, Queen 800, Police 500 and Thief 0 (or the names you grew up with), fold them the same way and toss them down. Keep a score sheet with one column per player.

Questions

Is Raja Rani Chor Police the same game as Cops and Robbers?

Yes. It is the same four-chit game. India calls it Raja Rani Chor Police or Chor Sipahi, Pakistan Raja Wazir Chor Sipahi, Nepal Raja Mantri Chor Sipahi, the USA and UK Cops and Robbers, Germany Räuber und Gendarm, and so on. ChitPlay plays it under the name of the language you choose.

How many players do you need?

Four chits are used, so four players. In ChitPlay computer players fill any empty seats, so you can play alone or with one to three friends.

How many points is each chit worth?

King 1000, Queen 800, Police 500 and Thief 0. The Police keeps 500 for catching the Thief; a wrong guess gives those 500 to the Thief.

What are house rules?

Twists from other countries that you can switch on: an innocent player cleared before the guess (Japan), a second guess for half points (USA), a 200-point penalty for a wrong guess (UK) and a 200-point fine for a caught thief (China).
