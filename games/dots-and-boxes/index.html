Dots and Boxes is a pen-and-paper classic from the 1800s, played in school notebooks everywhere. Players take turns drawing lines between dots, and whoever closes a box claims it. It looks childishly simple until the board fills up and every move starts giving something away.

How to play

  1. Take turns drawing one line between two neighbouring dots.
  2. If your line completes the fourth side of a box, it's yours, and you must draw again.
  3. The game ends when every line has been drawn.

Scoring

Each box you close is worth one point.

Who wins

The player with the most boxes wins. If everyone is level, it's a draw.

Tips

  • Avoid drawing the third side of a box: the next player just takes it.
  • Late in the game the board breaks into long chains of boxes. Try to make the other player open the first long chain.
  • Sometimes it pays to leave the last two boxes of a chain, so the other player has to open the next one.
