Snakes and Ladders grew from Moksha Patam, an old Indian board game about virtue and fortune. Today it's the simplest of races: roll, move, climb the ladders and slide down the snakes. Anyone can play it, at any age, and the lead can change on the very last roll. The board in ChitPlay is our own layout.

How to play

  1. Everyone starts off the board, before square 1. Players take turns to roll the die.
  2. Move your token forward by the number rolled, following the numbers up the board.
  3. Land at the foot of a ladder and you climb to its top. Land on a snake's head and you slide down to its tail.
  4. A six gives you another roll.
  5. Square 100 needs the exact number. If the roll would take you past it, your token stays where it is.

Scoring

There are no points during play. The result table shows the square each player reached.

Who wins

The first player to land exactly on square 100 wins.

House rules you can switch on

  • Start with a 6: A token waits off the board until its player rolls a 6. The 6 puts it on square 1, and that player rolls again.
  • Bounce back: A roll that goes past 100 bounces back: from 97, a 5 goes to 100 and back two squares to 98. Snakes and ladders still count where it lands.
  • Three sixes: Three sixes in a row send the token back to where it was at the start of that turn, and the turn passes on.

Tips

  • There are no decisions in this game, so it's a good one to play while chatting.
  • Nearing the end, every roll can matter: watch out for the snakes in the nineties.

Questions

Do you have to land exactly on 100?

Yes. If your roll is too high you stay where you are and try again next turn.

Is the game all luck?

Yes, entirely. That's why players of any age or skill can win.
