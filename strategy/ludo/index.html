Ludo strategy: how to win more often

Updated 2026-11-15

Ludo looks like a game of pure luck: you roll, you move. But watch two players with the same rolls and the better one finishes first almost every time. The dice decide how far you can go; you decide which token goes there, and that choice, made forty or fifty times a game, is where Ludo is won.

These tips start with the habits that matter most and end with the finer points. They work on a cardboard board at home and in ChitPlay, in Normal mode (first to bring all four tokens home) and in Score mode (most points when the ten-minute clock runs out).

1. Get two tokens out early

One token on the track gives you no choice: every roll has to move it, even into danger. With two out, you can always pick the safer move. Use your first sixes to bring tokens out until you have two in play; after that, a six is often better spent moving a token that is already running.

2. Count the squares behind you

A rival token one to six squares behind you can capture you with a single roll. Before every move, look behind the square you are about to land on. One token behind you means about a one-in-six chance of being caught next turn; two or three tokens behind you and it becomes likely. Land in front of an empty stretch of track whenever you can.

3. Use the safe squares

Start squares and the star squares cannot be captured. A token parked on a star is a token that cannot be sent home. When a rival is close behind, stepping onto a star is often worth more than racing ahead.

4. Chase captures that are close, not far

A capture sends a rival token all the way back and gives you another roll, so it is worth a lot. But a token chasing a target six or more squares away is usually just wandering into danger. Go for captures you can make this turn; otherwise, play safe and let the rivals come to you.

5. Do not leave a token at home forever

A token that never leaves the yard still has to make the whole journey later, when the board is crowded and every square is dangerous. Bring all four out over the course of the game rather than racing one token home and starting again from nothing.

6. Keep a token near your start

A token sitting just ahead of your start square can punish any rival who walks past it. Players often keep one token in this guard position for a while, especially when two or three rivals have to pass that stretch to get home.

7. Move the token in most danger first

When no move captures and no move reaches home, ask which of your tokens is most likely to be hit next turn, and move that one out of reach or onto a star. Moving the safest token is the most common mistake in Ludo.

8. The home column is safe: use it as a bank

Once a token turns into its home column nobody can touch it. Tokens there are banked progress. Late in the game, it can be smart to stop a token just inside the column and use your rolls on others, then bring it in with the exact number later.

9. Plan for exact rolls at the end

Reaching home needs the exact number. A token three squares from home wastes every roll that is not a three. Try to keep your last tokens at different distances from home, so more numbers are useful to you.

10. Watch the leader

In a four-player game, the player closest to finishing is everyone’s problem. If a capture is available on the leader and on someone far behind, take the leader. Slowing the leader down is often worth more than a few squares of your own.

11. Score mode: points over speed

In Score mode every square, every capture (+20) and every token home (+30) scores, and the game ends when the clock runs out. Captures are worth far more than they are in Normal mode, so take every safe one. Near the end of the clock, a capture or a token reaching home can swing the result; long safe walks matter less.

12. With friends: move in time

In ChitPlay, playing with people online or in a Play with Friends party, each move has five seconds. If the time runs out the computer moves for you and one of your three lifelines is gone; miss three turns and you are counted as offline and out of the game. Decide quickly: the safest-move habit above makes fast decisions easy.

Questions

Is Ludo just luck?

The rolls are luck, but which token to move is a real decision made dozens of times a game. Good players lose some games to bad rolls but win far more often over many games.

Should I always use a six to bring a token out?

Early on, yes, until you have two tokens in play. Later, a six can be better used to escape a threat, make a capture or reach home, since it also gives you another roll.

What are the safe squares in Ludo?

Each colour’s start square and the star squares. Tokens on them cannot be captured.
